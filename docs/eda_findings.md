# EDA Findings

## Status And Scope

- Project: Electricity Retail Sales Decision Intelligence
- Reviewed: 2026-09-17
- Period: 2016-01 through 2025-12
- Input: `data/interim/eia861m_retail_sales_2016_2025_with_quality_flags.csv`
- Unit of observation: state x month x end-use sector, including the District of Columbia
- Status: Descriptive findings and all four figures reviewed; ready for dashboard design

These findings describe published EIA-861M electricity retail sales data. They do not measure the performance of an individual utility or establish causal effects.

## Findings

| Question | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| How did the national market change? | Annual sales rose from 3,762.46 to 4,058.01 TWh from 2016 to 2025 (+7.9%). Nominal revenue rose from 386.51 to 553.28 billion USD (+43.1%). | Revenue increased much faster than sales in the published series. These values are not adjusted for inflation. |
| How did aggregate price move? | `100 * total revenue / total sales` increased from 10.27 to 13.63 nominal cents/kWh between 2016 and 2025. | This is implied average revenue per kWh, not a tariff or evidence that price changes caused sales changes. |
| Which sectors contributed most in 2025? | RES represented 37.3% of sales and 47.4% of revenue; COM 36.8% and 36.2%; IND 25.7% and 16.2%; TRA about 0.2% of each. | Sector sales and revenue shares differ. These are shares of the EIA national totals, not individual company market shares. |
| Which states contributed most in 2025? | Texas represented 12.8% of national sales, followed by Florida at 6.4% and California at 5.9%. | State contribution identifies scale for monitoring, not a causal driver or investment ranking. |
| Which state-sector combinations led 2025 sales? | TX-IND, TX-COM, and TX-RES were the three largest combinations, at about 175.0, 173.8, and 170.9 TWh, respectively. | A state can matter through several sectors; the ranking is descriptive and does not imply utility-level opportunity. |
| Is there a recurring within-year pattern? | In the annual-normalized profile, RES averaged 1.31 in July and 1.28 in August versus 0.77 in April. COM and IND had smaller summer peaks. | An index of 1.0 equals that sector's average month within the same year. Gray annual lines show broadly recurring patterns, but a forecasting baseline has not been evaluated. |

The 2025 average monthly customer count across the reported state-sector observations was about 164.51 million. Summing its 12 monthly values gives customer-months, not unique annual customers. The earlier `sector_totals` table covers all of 2016-2025; its shares should not be confused with the 2025 shares above.

## Selected Monitoring KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Grain |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Sum of published `sales` | Month or year; national, sector, or state |
| Nominal revenue | Sum of published `revenue` | Same as sales |
| Implied average price | `100 * sum(revenue) / sum(sales)` where sales is positive | Same as sales; cents/kWh |
| Average monthly customers | Mean of monthly customer counts for a yearly view | National, sector, state, or state-sector year |
| Annual growth | Current annual sales or revenue divided by prior year's value, minus one | Year and chosen segment |
| Sector contribution | Sector sales or revenue divided by the national total for the same period | Year or month |
| State contribution | State sales divided by national sales for the same period | Year or month |
| Seasonal sales index | Monthly sector sales divided by that sector-year's average monthly sales, then averaged across years | Sector x calendar month |

Do not use a sum of monthly customer counts as an annual customer KPI or a simple mean of state prices as a national price KPI.

## Data Quality And Limits

- All 2,646 observations with at least one zero metric were in TRA; 2,642 had all four main metrics equal to zero. Published zeros were retained.
- One small negative revenue value, DC-TRA in 2022-02, was retained and flagged.
- The public API does not identify which individual observations were reported, estimated, imputed, or adjusted.
- EIA-861M is a public monthly statistical series, not transaction data for every utility. TRA is the EIA end-use electricity sector, not all transportation activity.
- Revenue and implied price here are not inflation-adjusted. The figures cannot establish policy impacts, price elasticity, or business value generated by this project.
